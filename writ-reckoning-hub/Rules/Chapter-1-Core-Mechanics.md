### Background / Origin Stories

In the age before variance, there was one Power above all others.  It was the highest power because it guarded the geode of possibility.  The Geode was a universe sized Star where every possibility was kept crystalized.  The Warden as they became known, began to dole out certain crystals to lesser powers.  Creating small experiments, regulated worlds, and small demonstrations.  This was the Age of Regulation.  



 Over the Eons, these crystals have found their way into the mortal realms. Whether through theft, or dissemination, mortality now found opportunities to wield these powers of creation themselves.  Those who chose to do so were known as Summoners.

### Vessels and Forms

Vessels and forms are how the the players will harness their magic.  Characters connect with a vessel at an early time in their development.  For those raised in the practice, it usually happens in childhood.  For those who find their way in later in life, it usually only takes a few weeks of meditation to find their vessel.  Vessels can take almost any shape as long as they are able to contain Forms.  Forms are the physical manifestations of Shards of Creation.  For the most part, Forms appear in our world as Small multi-colored gemstones.  A character with even limited training can see the form inside just by looking at it. 



Some examples may include; a small leather bag with a family crest embroidered on the side,  a tiara with places to inset gems, a cross-body strap where gems can be sewn into.



As long as the character can maintain a mostly positive relationship with the Powers, their Vessels tend to be indestructible. (The characters themselves on the other hand tend to not be indestructible).  Some long standing Families pass down heirloom Vessel’s from generation to generation.  



Forms in Writ & Reckoning represent the real-life trading cards used for your character.  Your character will utilize these cards as their spells and majority of your actions in your adventure.

## Character Stats

These 6 stats are what measure the differences between adventures.  It is a measure both of how good they are at certain things at the best of times AND a measure of how proficient they feel in the moment.  For each of the 6 stats a character will have a “Dice Pool”.  These represent the possibility of success for that stat.  Each stat also has a Dice Pool Max.  This is the most dice you can roll at any given time for that stat.  It is possible to more dice in your dice pool than you max. We’ll get more into why that difference exists and what it means in later sections regarding soulrocks.

### Command

Command is a character’s ability to Lead other people.  It represents both their extroverted nature, charisma, and their strategic thinking.  This stat is a measure of how your character can handle other sentient beings, whether that’s managing friendships, leading combat forces on the battlefield, navigating tense negotiations, or simply making a good first impression.  



Command based characters are sometimes referred to as “Concordants”. Especially if they are summoners who mainly use other summoned creatures in combat.  Staying back and organizing strategic attacks from a distance.

### Imagination

Imagination is a character’s ability to think.  Not just any thoughts either, but it is a measure of creativity, ponderance, and mental resilience. What are the odds your character constantly has unique thoughts, or comes up with things no one else would, the higher the chance of that, the higher the imagination.  Since forms are fragments of potential reality,  some of the more abstract forms (spells and such) require a high imagination to creatively and effectively use.  Unlike forms with more straightforward appearances (creatures, equipment).  



Outside of combat and summoning,  imagination is used to spot inconsistencies, form theories, or understand complex principles.

### Might

Might is one of 2 physical traits.  Might is a measure of raw strength and endurance.  Typically showcased by resisting disease, lifting fallen trees, or most commonly, just smashing doors open.

It’s nearly impossible to talk about Might without talking finesse as they are counterparts to a character’s physical abilities.  Might is the quantity of strength.  Finesse is the Quality.  High might with low finesse is a raging berserker.  Crushing anything and everything but with zero control of side effects, bystanders, accidental casualties, etc.  



Might is also more associated with endurance while finesse is related to skill.  Might can run a marathon, but finesse can play a piano concerto.

### Finesse

Finesse is the other physical trait and leans toward coordination more than strength.  Finesse measures a characters ability to dance, pick locks, and walk across a narrow tightrope.



High Finesse with low might, means that your character is fluid, graceful, and has full control of their body at all times.  But when push comes to shove, they will get shoved, hard.

### Craft

Craft is a simple and complicated measure.  It is simply put, an understanding of engineering.  It is not just knowledge but also ability to create things.  Tinkering, building, and wielding weaponry, armor, and tools are fundamental here.  But a high Craft stat also represents an understanding of the limits of creation and an acceptance of their eventual decay or destruction.

### Essence

Essence is a measure of connection.  Since the natural world grows around you with or without your meddling, essence measures a character’s connection to all other life.  Including the spirits within the elemental forces that create the reality and world you live in.

### Health

Your character's Health represents their physical resilience, endurance, and ability to withstand harm. Unlike many systems that use a flat number, Writ & Reckoning uses **Health Dice** to create natural variation and make recovery more dynamic.

**Step 1: Base Health**

Every character starts with 6 Base Health. This represents basic human vitality before any other factors.

**Step 2: Vessel Type Health Modifier**

Your Vessel's physical nature affects how much protection it provides. Different types of Vessels offer varying degrees of health benefits:

Worn Vessels (+3 Health)

Vessels that are worn on the body provide maximum protection through constant contact and defensive positioning. Forms can be embedded directly into the item's structure.

Examples: Armor with gem sockets, protective masks with inlaid stones, enchanted cloaks with sewn-in Forms, reinforced bracers, ceremonial robes

Held Vessels (+2 Health)

Vessels designed to be actively wielded provide moderate protection through their structural integrity and the bearer's ability to use them defensively.

Examples: Staff with crystal head, sword with gem-socketed hilt, shield with embedded Forms, war hammer with stone inlays, bow with crystalline grip

Carried Vessels (+1 Health)

Vessels that are transported but not worn provide minimal direct protection, though their proximity still offers some resilience.

Examples: Gemstone-lined bags, crystal-embedded books, ornate boxes with Form slots, decorated instruments with stone inlays, lanterns with crystalline panels

Bound Vessels (+0 Health)

Vessels that exist as separate entities or are spiritually rather than physically connected to the bearer offer no direct physical protection.

Examples: Familiar creatures (bonded animals, spirits), living plants under your care, ancestral totems that remain in sacred places, elemental contracts

**Step 3: Background Modifier**

Your life before becoming wielding your Vessel shaped your physical resilience:

Sheltered (nobles, scholars, artisans): +1 Health, 200 starting coin

Working (farmers, merchants, crafters): +2 Health, 100 starting coin

Hardened (soldiers, survivors, laborers): +3 Health, 50 starting coin

**Step 4: Scars and Injuries (Optional)**

You may choose to take old wounds or lasting injuries to gain benefits elsewhere:

Minor Scar (-1 Health): +1 to any stat maximum or +50 starting coin

Significant Injury (-2 Health): +2 to any stat maximum or +100 starting coin

Major Trauma (-3 Health): +3 to any stat maximum or +150 starting coin

**Step 5: Determine Health Dice**

Your total Health determines your Health Dice type:

1-3 Health: no Health Dice (Vulnerable)

4-7 Health: d4 Health Dice (Fragile)

8-11 Health: d6 Health Dice (Average)

12-15 Health: d8 Health Dice (Resilient)

16+ Health: d10 Health Dice (Exceptional)

**Using Health Dice**

**Recovery and Rest**

When you rest, you don't simply regain a flat amount of Health. Instead, you roll your Health Dice:

Short Rest (1 hour): Roll 1 Health Die, regain that much Health

Long Rest (8 hours): Roll 2 Health Dice, regain that much Health

Extended Rest (24+ hours): Roll 3 Health Dice, regain that much Health

### Defense

Your defense score is a rating of how good you are at avoiding damage.  Your defense style is on of your innate abilities and determines the method by which you avoid damage.  Maybe you use your flexibility to dodge, your armor to block, or magic to divert incoming damage.  No matter what you do, your character will depend on that method to avoid the dangers of the world.  Each defensive style includes instructions on how to grow in your ability, as well as certain advantages or limitations of training that way.  (i.e armored bulwarks can craft new armor easier, or imbue their armor with extra strength, but probably suck at sneaking and being quiet.).  Not every defensive style is perfect and usually comes with a weakness (see damage types).

Defensive Abilities can be upgraded with Adjectives just like other abilities.

## Dice Rolling and Skill Checks

### The D6

#### Dice Pools, Max Stats, and Modifiers

### Dice Pools

Each Stat has a “Dice Pool”.  These represent the possibility of success for that stat.  Each stat also has a Dice Pool Max.  This is the most dice you can roll at any given time for that stat.  It is possible to more dice in your dice pool than you max.

#### Exploding 6’s

Whenever you roll a 6 on a d6 from a dice pool. It counts as a success BUT ALSO, you get to reroll it.  If you get a 6 (and only a 6) on the second roll, you get a bonus success.  This can be continued infinitely as long as you keep rolling 6’s.

### Modifier Dice

#### D4, D6, and D8. Clarity vs. Strain

Sometimes your character will have a clear shot at success, a task so practiced and rehearsed that they could do it in their sleep.  And sometimes, the adventure has taken it’s toll to the point where even normally easy thing have become hard.

Wielding a Vessel already comes with fluctuating abilities.  And besides the dice pool changing, Game Masters also have the opportunity to offer rolls with Clarity, or Strain.  All Skill checks and attack rolls have a modifier dice that is roll alongside your normal pool of d6’s.  This modifier dice should look different since it is by default also a d6. Unlike Dice pool d6’s, your modifier die is a straight number, not a success or fail.  Meaning that if you roll a 4, that adds 4 to your overall roll.



Example: Gracy McFangtooth has a Dice Pool Max of 4 in Might.  When Rolling a Might check he would roll 4d6 and count the successes.  Then roll a d6 Modifier Dice and add the number.  Lets say he rolls a 2 successes and a 4 on the Mod die.  That is a total of 6!



When rolling with Clarity, your mod die changes to a d8, and conversely when rolling with Strain its a d4.  Notably when rolling with Clarity, even if you roll a 1, it’s not considered a critical fail.  And you guessed it,  even if you roll a 4 on the mod die when Strained, it’s not a critical hit.

#### Critical Hits / Critical Fails

Critical rolls happen when you roll the maximum or minimum number on a Modifier Die.  When rolling with Clarity or Strain, only one extreme is possible (no critical success with strain, and no critical fail with clarity).



But what does getting a critical roll mean? Inside of combat a critical roll when trying to hit a single target means double damage. For AOE attacks, non-damaging attacks (i.e. attempting to poison a foe), or other situations a critical roll  will at minimum, definitely succeed.  GM’s are allowed to reward/punish critical rolls with added side effects if they wish.

For basic skill checks like climbing a wall, reading ancient texts, or avoiding a trap, It can mean automatically succeeding or failing at that task.  Depending on your total (including your dice pool roll) a GM may rule that a critical fail doesn’t mean absolute failure but instead a secondary effect happens.  (when climbing the wall you get to the top but slice your leg on a piece of rusty metal on the way up).



Same for critical successes, You climb the wall and find a copper piece lucky side up at the top.



This should be discussed in session zero between GM’s and players so everyone can expect the same level of chaos from critical rolls.  After a table settles on a ruling, It’s important that the GM maintain consistency when ruling on critical rolls.

## Forms of Power | Cards

### Card Types

#### Creatures/Allies

Ally cards summon living entities to fight, assist, or interact with the world on behalf of the summoner. These are not illusions but copies of souls, embodied and brought forth through chaos magic.

##### Combat and Actions

HP & Damage: Allies have HP and Damage values but do not have a set Defense score.

Defending: When attacked, Ally creatures use their controllers command score as their defense score.

Attacking: When an ally attacks, the summoner rolls Command to determine effectiveness.

Targeting: Allies can be targeted by anyone, including their summoner, other party members, other summoned allies, and enemies.

##### What Allies Can Do

Allies can take any reasonable action, such as: Attacking an enemy, moving around the battlefield, or interacting with objects (pushing, pulling, opening, carrying).

However, their abilities are subject to common sense and DM discretion:

If an ally has no attack value (e.g., a 0/3 ally from another game), it cannot attack.

A fish-like ally may not be able to pull a lever if it lacks hands or tentacles.

The DM may require a Command check to determine if an ally can perform a complex action.

##### Dismissing Allies

Allies disappear immediately upon reaching 0 HP..

Healing is possible before they disappear..

If an ally was wielding an item (such as armor or weapons from an Item Card), those items remain behind for others to pick up. However, magical buffs or enchantments tied specifically to the ally fade immediately.

If a controlling player is not actively maintaining the summon, the ally automatically disappears.

#### Spells

Spell cards contain refined and stored magic, locked into controlled effects by ancient scholars and wizards. Despite this, spells remain chaotic in nature, allowing summoners to bend their effects to fit their needs.

##### Casting Spells

Spells take effect immediately upon being summoned unless they create a lasting effect (such as buffs, enchantments, or traps).

Most spell cards do not list a range. Instead, the summoner must pay AP based on the distance to the target (See AP Cost Table).

#### Equipment

Item cards summon physical objects that were touched, copied, and sometimes enhanced by chaos magic. These could be weapons, armor, potions, or tools, and unlike spells, they persist in the world instead of activating instantly.

##### Using Items

Items must be worn, wielded, or used to gain their effects.

Summoners who specialize in items can summon them directly onto allies, instantly equipping them (See Summoning Range).

##### Trading, Dropping, and Losing Items

Summoned items exist physically only as long as the controller maintains them—if control is lost, they disappear.

If a summoned item is destroyed, it simply disappears.

##### Equipping and Passing Items

If the summoner summons an item directly onto themselves or an ally, it is immediately equipped.

If an item is handed off after summoning, AP may be required for the transfer.

Example: Tossing a sword to another player requires AP, determined by the DM based on distance and difficulty.

An Item cannot be permanently destroyed.  If it would be destroyed by damage or effects it simply is dismissed.

#### Elements/Nature/Essence

Essence Cards are the purest form of chaos magic and the most versatile of all cards. They represent the natural forces of the world, from fire and water to life and death.  They even encompass forms of energy like love, song, and emotions.

### Card Tiers

We recommend that GM’s prepare a set of cards before the campaign based on the planned narrative, environment,  player characters, backgrounds, etc.  In addition, it will be necessary to separate those cards into tiers.

Separating cards into tiers is necessary since there is not innate cost differences to the cards.  While things like Magic the gathering cards may have a cost value on the card, pokemon, uno, or tarot cards do not.  To facilitate balancing effects that could break a campaign in the early stages, the following classification system will aid dm’s in locking powerful cards behind upgrades for the characters to work towards:

#### Card Tier Table

Tiers range from 1-5.  For creatures the tier is based on total health + damage.  Most creatures in any game will have both an offensive and defensive number.  These should be mathematical converted depending on the game to a number that is the same relative scale.

Card games that usually operate in the tens (60, 90, 100) would be divided by ten.

Games that operate in the thousands (1000, 1850, 2500) would be divided by 1000 (and then rounded).

Games that already operate in the ones (1, 5, 3) usually don’t need to change.

It is imperative that whatever translation you choose is consistent for ALL cards from that game.

Tier 1 should usually not do more than 3 damage and not have more than 5 health.

Tier 2 would usually be around 4-6 damage and <7 health.

So on and so forth with tier 5 having no limits.

GM’s are allowed discretion in allowing things that have higher numbers into a tier if say the card has a significant drawback or counteracting weakness.  Allowing players to take a risk with summoning that form.

Spell cards should be judged on damage as well.  For spell cards that don’t do direct damage, the GM will have to personally rule on how powerful the effect could be (see interpreting card effects)

Equipment usually can follow the same rules as creatures since most equipments either will deal extra damage, gain temporary health, or some other numerically definable trait.

Essence Cards are unique in that they usually either have a basic version or an advanced version with limited space between.  GM’s once again should use their best judgement.

## Soulrocks

### Lore - Appearance

Those who wield Vessels are natural in the world, but their power, gifted from the higher powers is fundamentally foreign. This means that as a character uses their powers the magic mixed with their own emotions, causes a reaction.  DEFAULT: this causes small red barnacle looking growths to form along their forearms.  They can be painlessly peeled off to be used in communion rituals.

Variation on this could be in color, shape, or how they appear. See our Custom World-Building Doc.

### How to Gain them

Soulrocks are gained on any critical success of if the GM determines that a moment was significant in terms of either character growth or would greatly please the higher powers (or the power associated with the player).  Soulrocks should be gained on average 1-3 times per player per session.

Gained soulrocks are stored in your stats (see character sheet).  They are represented by d6’s that you place in a certain stat.  If you have more than your max for that stat you still roll only as many as your stat max will allow.  If you have less than your max, you roll only the dice you have.

## Character Death

When a character is reduced to zero hp they are at death’s door.  The Player has a few options in this moment and ultimately should have control of what happens.  The first option is obvious,  they can let their character die, GM’s should allow for an uttering of final words, or some other emotional roleplay moment if the situation allows.  In more intense combat situations, a player may be permitted to take a minor (2 AP or less) action as a final act of courage.  



If a character’s body is not in immediate danger, they may choose to pay 1 soulrock to postpone their decision 1 turn.  (possibly giving their friends time to heal them or help).  



When the time to choose comes, if a player does not want to have their character die permanently, they have another option: the Summoner’s Bargain.

#### The Summoner’s Bargain – Player Options

**Upon reaching 0 HP, the player must choose:**

1 - Lose a Card at Random – The player randomly discards one card from their collection and stabilizes at 1 HP.

This card is permanently gone—its magic consumed to keep the summoner alive. (Possibly donated to the Higher Power at Death’s Door as a bribe)

The DM may allow the player to earn back an equivalent card later through quests or story events.

Good for players who prefer mechanical loss over character changes.

**OR**

2 - Suffer a Lasting Wound or Penalty – The summoner stabilizes at 1 HP but gains a permanent physical or magical wound.

In low-stakes situations, this can be purely aesthetic (e.g., losing an eye, gaining a large scar).

In high-stakes situations, the penalty should be a real gameplay challenge:

Losing a leg → Must use a wheelchair, prosthetic, or mount to move.

Losing both eyes → Must rely on a familiar, allies, or blind-fighting techniques.

Some wounds may reduce Core Stats.

#### Guidelines for DMs: Keeping It Fair & Fun

- Let the Player Choose – If they pick random card loss, honor that choice. If they pick a wound, work with them to find something that’s narratively interesting but still playable.

- Use the Penalty Table (Below) – Instead of assigning a wound randomly, let the player roll to avoid feeling targeted.

- Severity Increases with Multiple 0 HP Events – If a player drops to 0 HP multiple times in one encounter, the Bargain gets worse each time (e.g., first time is an eye, second time is a leg, third time they might be beyond saving).  Players should record how many bargains they’ve made on their character sheet.

- A Player Can Choose to Die – If a player feels their story has reached a natural conclusion, they may choose to die instead of taking the Bargain. This is their choice, not the DM’s.

## Combat

### Turn order and the Center of combat

#### Determining the Center of Combat

Unlike traditional initiative systems, Writ & Reckoning uses a narrative-driven approach to determine turn order. At the start of combat, the DM establishes the Center of Combat—the focal point around which the battle or conflict revolves. This could be a powerful enemy, an artifact, a battlefield objective, or even an environmental hazard. The Center of Combat should reflect the stakes of the encounter and encourage player engagement with the battlefield. For example, in a brawl over a stolen relic, the Center of Combat might be the relic itself, forcing players to consider their positioning. In a fight against a rampaging beast, the beast might be the center—unless the players provoke it into focusing on something else. The key is that the Center of Combat is not static; it changes as the battle evolves to reflect the flow of action.

#### Determining Turn Order

Turn order is determined by proximity to the Center of Combat—whoever is closest goes first. This means characters near the focal point of battle act before those on the outskirts. This system naturally rewards aggressive or tactical positioning, ensuring that those engaged in the fight are reacting first while distant players must spend AP to close the gap.

#### Breaking Ties

If multiple players or enemies are equally close to the Center of Combat, the DM can break the tie in one of the following ways:

1.Highest AP Pool: The character with the most remaining AP acts first.

2. D6 Roll-Off: A quick roll-off where the highest number wins.

3. Narrative Logic: The DM may decide turn order based on who was most prepared to act (e.g., an enemy ambushing from the shadows might act before a hesitant player).

#### When to Change the Center of Combat

The Center of Combat is fluid—and can shift when the battle’s focus shifts. Some examples:

If an enemy escapes and the players chase them down, the Center of Combat could move with the chase.

If a powerful spell reshapes the battlefield, the Center of Combat may move to the newly created hazard.

If a player picks up the battle’s objective (like a magic crystal), the Center of Combat could follow them.

The DM should reassess the Center of Combat as needed, ensuring it continues to highlight the most engaging part of the fight while keeping the flow of battle dynamic and engaging.

### Action Points

Action Points (AP) represent a character’s ability to act within a turn. Every action—summoning cards, attacking, moving, and interacting with the battlefield—costs AP.

Each player has a pool of AP that resets at the start of their turn. The stronger a character becomes, the more AP they have to spend per turn.

#### What AP Represents

AP isn’t just stamina or magical energy—it’s a combination of reaction time, focus, and effort. A player with high AP can do more in a single turn, while someone low on AP must be more selective with their choices.

#### Spending AP

All Actions (when in combat) have an Action Cost.   Everything from summoning a card, attacking with a weapon, moving, to maintaining summons and effects over time.  Most actions have a base cost with additional effects needing more Action Energy.  



Summoning a card for example will cost more depending on the Tier of the card.  The amount of distance your character wants to move will affect the cost.

Higher-level characters will have larger AP pools, allowing them to perform more actions per turn. However, careful management is key—spending AP too fast can leave a player vulnerable!
