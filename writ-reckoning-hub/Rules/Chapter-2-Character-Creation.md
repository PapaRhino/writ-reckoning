# Chapter 2: Character Creation

Source: Writ & Reckoning Official Sourcebook V2.2 (October 2025).

## Starting Out

Most campaigns will all start at the beginning.  (see Notes on starting at higher growth points for end-game based campaigns).  Since Writ & Reckoning doesn’t have levels, each campaign and character will have a unique pace and style to the growth they experience.

When starting out, there are a few things that players will need to come prepared with. Each character will already have summoning abilities, a vessel, and at least one form. Most characters will also start with an innate ability.  



There are no classes in Writ & Reckoning unlike other TTRPG’s. This means that uniqueness between characters is up to the endless customization available when creating and upgrading characters.

When creating your character you will be be given a number of creation points.  You will use these points to determine your starting stats, innate abilities, starting cards (forms), health, Defense Score and starting equipment.

## Natural Born Abilities

There are many innate abilities that players can choose from when creating their character.  Some abilities have prereqs either in stats or affinity.  These prereqs are not there to limit your choices but rather there to avoid complications when trying to resolve things later on.  Feel free to work with your GM to customize or change an ability but have caution.  Most prereqs have explanations as to why they shouldn’t be changed.

Each Character starts with 4 abilities.  One is related to your vessel of choice, one is your defensive style (how you avoid taking damage from enemies and the world around you), and one is related to your affinity with magic and higher powers.  The last category is for abilities relating to your physical self (tails, wings, claws, hair made of snakes, etc).

Innate Abilities also can be upgraded later on. See upgrade table in Spending Soulrocks.

### Vessel Abilities

These abilities will be direct boons based on wielding a specific vessel.  Usually related to the related Higher or Lesser Power that created the vessel.  This also determines the card type of your first starting form.

### Defensive Styles

How you avoid damage.  Common examples are Armored Bulwark, Dexterous Dodging, Mental Diversion, etc.  Each comes with upgradable abilities, features and weaknesses.

### Innate Powers

Can be anything from telepathy to firebreath.  A sort of magic that you yourself posses separate from the forms.  Since you yourself are a form in the grande geode of possibility.

### Physical Traits

Claws, wings, tails, etc.  Things that you were born with or grew as part of your physical manifestation.

## Affinity to Higher Powers

Each Character will start with an affinity to one of the 4 major powers.  This is usually based on your vessel type but for those with vessels from lesser powers it may be associated with your highest stat.  



Affinity for a certain Major Power will make future upgrades, petitions, and transformations easier when working with that power. Characters may change their affinity later on if they change vessels, or if their highest stat changes.

## Lesser Powers

Some Mortals have impressed the Higher powers to such an extent that they have been elevated to the status of Lesser Power.  These beings have a great deal of magic power and have been tasked with trying to create new Forms.  This usually means their boons are unstable and are still works-in progress.

## Designing Character Vessels

### Vessel Types and Affinities:

Vessels can take many styles.  The Higher Powers and some of the Lesser Powers have made vessels in many different ways over the eons. Vessels all have a story and most are ancient.



Types:

Worn Vessels

Vessels that are worn on the body provide maximum protection through constant contact and defensive positioning. Forms can be embedded directly into the item's structure.

Examples: Armor with gem sockets, protective masks with inlaid stones, enchanted cloaks with sewn-in Forms, reinforced bracers, ceremonial robes

Held Vessels

Vessels designed to be actively wielded provide moderate protection through their structural integrity and the bearer's ability to use them defensively.

Examples: Staff with crystal head, sword with gem-socketed hilt, shield with embedded Forms, war hammer with stone inlays, bow with crystalline grip

Carried Vessels

Vessels that are transported but not worn provide minimal direct protection, though their proximity still offers some resilience.

Examples: Gemstone-lined bags, crystal-embedded books, ornate boxes with Form slots, decorated instruments with stone inlays, lanterns with crystalline panels

Bound Vessels

Vessels that exist as separate entities or are spiritually rather than physically connected to the bearer offer no direct physical protection.

Examples: Familiar creatures (bonded animals, spirits), living plants under your care, ancestral totems that remain in sacred places, elemental contracts



Each Vessel also has an affinity (the Power responsible for making it).
