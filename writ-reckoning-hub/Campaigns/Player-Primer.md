# How to Play Writ & Reckoning in 5 Minutes

A one-page primer for players. No GM secrets in here — just what you need to play tonight.

## The idea in one sentence

You are a Summoner: a person with a vessel (a cloak, a hammer, a book, a satchel — something that holds Forms) and a handful of real trading cards that are your spells, creatures, and gear. You bring the actual cards to the table, lay them down, and they do what they say — translated into this game's numbers by your GM.

## Your six stats

- **Command** — leading people and creatures, negotiating, reading a room.
- **Imagination** — thinking, inventing, understanding magic and strange things.
- **Might** — raw strength and endurance.
- **Finesse** — coordination, dodging, sneaking, picking locks.
- **Craft** — making, fixing, and understanding tools, gear, and mechanisms.
- **Essence** — connection to living things, nature, and the wild.

There are no levels and no classes. Your character grows by spending Soulrocks (see below), not by leveling up.

You also have **Health** (how much harm you can take) and **Defense** (how good you are at avoiding harm — dodging, blocking, or misdirecting, depending on your style).

## Rolling dice: pools, exploding 6s, and the modifier die

Every stat has a **dice pool**: a number of six-sided dice. If your Command is 4, you roll 4d6 for a Command check. Each pool die that shows **4 or more is a success**.

**Exploding 6s:** a 6 is a success AND you reroll it. Roll another 6 and you get a bonus success — and you reroll again. Keep going while the 6s keep coming. A lucky roll can turn a hopeless check into a triumph.

Beside your pool, you always roll **one modifier die** — a different-colored die, so it stands out. Normally it is a d6, and whatever number it shows is added straight to your successes. So: successes + modifier die = your total. The GM will tell you the difficulty: Easy 3, Standard 5, Hard 7, or Heroic 9. Meet it or beat it, and you succeed.

**Clarity and Strain:** sometimes the GM says you roll with Clarity (your modifier die becomes a d8 — a practiced task, a good tool, a solid plan) or with Strain (it becomes a d4 — you are exhausted, frightened, or fighting in the dark). Clarity can never critically fail; Strain can never critically succeed.

**Criticals:** roll the highest number on your modifier die and you critically succeed; roll the lowest and you critically fail. In a fight, a critical success on a single target means **double damage**. Outside a fight, a critical success at least definitely succeeds, and a critical fail at least definitely fails — the GM may add a fun side effect, but it will be fair and consistent all night.

## Action Points (AP)

You get **6 AP on your turn**, and they come back at the start of your next turn. Everything costs AP:

- Summoning a card: Tier 1 costs 2 AP, Tier 2 costs 3, Tier 3 costs 4, Tier 4 costs 5, Tier 5 costs 6. (Your cards' tiers are written on your Forms sheet.)
- Moving: nearby = 1 AP, far away = 2 AP.
- Attacking with an ally or an item: 2 AP.
- Simple things (pull a lever, open a door, pick something up): 1 AP.
- Keeping a lasting effect going: 1 AP per turn.

A typical turn: summon one creature and attack with it, or move twice and attack, or attack three times with a creature you already summoned. Spend wisely — an empty turn is a vulnerable turn.

## Forms: your cards are your powers

Your Forms are real Pokémon trading cards, and they come in types:

- **Creatures / Allies** — living beings that fight and act for you. They have Health and Damage, but no Defense of their own: when someone attacks your ally, they use **your Command** as their Defense. When your ally attacks, **you roll Command** to see how well it does. Allies disappear if they reach 0 Health — heal them before that happens.
- **Spells** — instant magic. They happen right away, unless they create a lasting effect (like a light or a shield).
- **Equipment** — weapons, armor, tools. They only work while you hold, wear, or maintain them. If one would be destroyed, it simply vanishes instead.
- **Elements / Nature / Essence** — raw natural forces: fire, water, growth, light. The most flexible cards in the game.

Your character starts with a few Forms and room for more (usually a pool of 2–4 slots). You will find new Forms as you play. Cards from Pokémon are translated by dividing their numbers by 10 — a card with 60 Health and 30 damage becomes 6 Health and 3 damage in this game. Your Forms sheet already has the translated numbers; you just play what's written there.

## Combat: no initiative, just the Center

There is no initiative roll and no fixed turn order. Instead, the GM names the **Center of Combat** — the person, objective, or hazard the fight is really about (a villain, a relic, a doorway). Whoever is **closest to the Center acts first**. If two people are equally close, the one with more AP left goes first, then a quick d6 roll-off, then the GM decides by what makes sense.

The Center **moves** when the fight changes: if someone grabs the relic, the Center follows them; if the villain flees, the Center follows the chase. Listen for the GM announcing the Center — it tells you where the fight is, and where you should be.

## Soulrocks: the good stuff

When you critically succeed, or do something genuinely significant, the GM may give you a **Soulrock** — a small, red, barnacle-like growth from your own magic, peeled off and kept. Expect about 1–3 per player per session. Soulrocks are stored in your stats as extra dice (you still never roll more than your stat's maximum), and you spend them between scenes to grow:

- +1 Max Health: 2 Soulrocks
- +1 card slot: 5 Soulrocks
- +1 to a stat's dice pool maximum: 4 Soulrocks
- A new Feat or Ability: 9 Soulrocks
- Upgrading a card: 3 Soulrocks × the card's tier
- Raising your Defense style or your maximum card tier: 5, then 9, then 12, then 15 Soulrocks across successive upgrades

Spend them on what your character would actually want — that is the whole point of a game with no classes.

## If you hit 0 Health: the Summoner's Bargain

Reaching 0 Health does not automatically mean death. You choose:

1. **Die** — with final words, and possibly one last small act (2 AP or less) if the fight allows.
2. **Pay 1 Soulrock** to postpone the decision for one turn — giving your friends a chance to heal you.
3. **Take the Summoner's Bargain** — and stabilize at 1 Health. Either you lose one of your cards permanently (chosen at random), or you take a lasting wound or penalty (rolled on a table — anything from a glowing scar to a lost eye to a strange new quirk). The Bargain gets worse if you hit 0 Health repeatedly in the same fight.

It is your choice, always. A good party plans for this before it happens: who carries healing, who can reach a fallen friend, and what you are willing to lose.

## Resting

When you rest, you roll **Health Dice** to recover Health — the die depends on your Max Health (small characters roll a d4, sturdy ones a d8 or d10). A short rest (1 hour) rolls 1 die, a long rest (8 hours) rolls 2, and a full day's rest rolls 3. Your GM will tell you when a rest is safe — in a dangerous place, it usually is not.

## Tonight, in short

Roll your pool, count 4s and up, add your modifier die, beat the difficulty. Spend your 6 AP wisely. Stay near the Center of Combat. Protect your allies — they use your Command to survive. And when the dice explode, enjoy it: that is the game working exactly as intended.
