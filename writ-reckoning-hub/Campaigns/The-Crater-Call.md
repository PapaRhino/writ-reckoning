# The Crater Call

A Writ & Reckoning one-shot for 4 players, about 3–4 hours, in rustic-fantasy Paldea.

## Overview

### Premise

The Great Crater on Paldea's west side is dimming. Its bright rim ring still catches the light, but the light itself is thinning: the springs below the rim taste of iron, summoned allies flicker and fade near the mountain, and the wild creatures of the rim country are restless in a way the old wardens have never seen. The cause is a man, not a monster — Corvin Ash, a disgraced scholar-summoner, has rigged a cracked lodestone in a grotto beneath the rim and is draining the crater's shard of possibility, convinced he is saving it by owning it.

The party are traveling summoners hired — or simply moved — to find out why the crater is failing and stop the drain before the shard goes dark. Along the way they will meet a rival who wants the same shard for a purse, a warden who knows more than she first says, and a village that needs the mountain healthy again.

### Tone

Warm, adventurous, a little eerie. This is a swords-and-magic road story: thatched hamlets, footpaths, lantern light, old shrines, and one very polite villain who is ruining everything while believing he is rescuing it. Combat is real and can hurt, but no scene requires killing anyone — the rival retreats, the villain can surrender, and the wild creatures can be calmed as well as fought.

### Player count and characters

4 players, choosing 4 of the 5 pregens:

- Alba Ferrer — Command-focused summoner leader, Worn vessel (gem-socketed armor), affinity Reyer (creature shards). See `Characters/Alba.md` and `Cards/Alba-Forms.md`.
- Tomas Vidal — Might / Finesse physical fighter, Held vessel (gem-socketed war hammer), affinity Consnoc (equipment). See `Characters/Tomas.md` and `Cards/Tomas-Forms.md`.
- Luz Ortega — Craft / Imagination tinkerer-scholar, Carried vessel (crystal-embedded field journal), affinity Ginanig (spells). See `Characters/Luz.md` and `Cards/Luz-Forms.md`.
- Willa Bramble — Essence wanderer and healer, Worn vessel (herb-cloak), affinity Viveseviv (elements / nature). See `Characters/Willa-Bramble.md` and `Cards/Willa-Forms.md`.
- Jory Fenwick — Energy-shepherd scout, Carried vessel (messenger's satchel), affinity Verdant (Pokémon energy cards as an unstable form). See `Characters/Jory-Fenwick.md` and `Cards/Jory-Forms.md`.

Whichever 4 are chosen, the adventure works unchanged. If a group has 5 players, run all 5 pregens and add one extra crag hound in Scene 2 (HOUSE RULE adjustment, for balance only).

### What the GM needs

- This file, the five pregen sheets, and the Forms files listed above.
- Physical dice: a pile of d6s for every player (pools run up to 5d6), plus one differently colored die per player for the modifier die, and one d8 and one d4 per player for Clarity and Strain.
- The players' real Pokémon TCG cards for their Forms, where they have them. If a physical card is missing, use a plain token with the card's name and its converted HP / damage / effect written on it — the converted numbers in the Forms files govern, not the printed card.
- Something to track AP with: six tokens per character, spent as actions are taken and returned at the start of that character's turn. This is the single best table habit in this system — use it from the first fight.
- No battlemap is required. Distances in this adventure are near (within a room, a clearing, or a shouted conversation) or far (across a grotto, down a trail, out of easy reach). If you use a map, use it for position relative to the Center of Combat, not for counting squares.

### Running time

- Opening and Scene 1 (Alderford, social): about 45 minutes
- Scene 2 (the rim trail, exploration and combat): about 60 minutes
- Scene 3 (the rival at the Watcher's Shelf, social or combat): about 45 minutes
- Scene 4 (the Hollow Heart grotto, climax): about 60–75 minutes
- Total: about 3–4 hours, with a natural break point after Scene 2 (camp at the Shelf).

## GM guidance: how to run Writ & Reckoning at this table

### Dice pools with physical dice

Each stat has a dice pool (a number of d6s) and a dice pool max. A player rolls as many pool d6s as they have in that stat, up to the max — Soulrocks stored in a stat can push the pool above the max, but you only ever roll up to the max (sourcebook). Pool d6s succeed on 4+ (HOUSE RULE). A 6 on a pool d6 is a success AND is rerolled; a second 6 is a bonus success, chaining while 6s continue (sourcebook: exploding 6s).

Beside the pool, every check rolls one modifier die: a d6 by default, a d8 with Clarity, a d4 with Strain (sourcebook). The modifier die is a straight number added to successes. Check total = successes + modifier die number (HOUSE RULE).

- Rolling the maximum on the modifier die is a critical success; rolling the minimum is a critical fail (sourcebook). Clarity cannot crit-fail, Strain cannot crit-succeed (sourcebook).
- In combat, a single-target critical success means double damage (sourcebook). Otherwise, a critical success at minimum definitely succeeds, and a critical fail at minimum definitely fails — the GM may add a side effect, but keep it consistent all night (sourcebook, and agree the level of chaos with the table before Scene 1).
- Example, to read aloud once at the start: "Alba rolls Command 5d6 and gets 1, 4, 5, 6, 6. The 4 and 5 are successes, the two 6s are successes and reroll — say they come up 2 and 6, so one more success. That is 5 successes. Her modifier d6 shows 3. Total 8 — a Hard check (7) passes."

### Difficulties (HOUSE RULE)

Easy 3, Standard 5, Hard 7, Heroic 9. As a rule of thumb: ordinary village tasks are Easy, trail and grotto tasks are Standard, the siphon lines and the villain's wards are Hard, and anything that would end the story early is Heroic. Say the difficulty out loud before the roll whenever the characters could reasonably judge it — it keeps the game honest and fast.

### Center of Combat (sourcebook)

There is no initiative. At the start of every fight, name the Center of Combat — a person, an objective, or a hazard — and whoever is closest to it acts first. Ties break by highest remaining AP, then a d6 roll-off, then narrative logic (sourcebook). The Center shifts when the fight's focus shifts: if an enemy flees, the Center moves with the chase; if someone grabs the objective, the Center follows them. Each fight below names its starting Center and its likely shifts. Announce every shift out loud: "The Center moves to the lodestone rig — everyone near the rig goes before everyone at the door."

### AP tracking (HOUSE RULE)

Every character has 6 AP per turn, refreshing at the start of their turn. Costs: summon Tier 1 = 2 AP, Tier 2 = 3 AP, Tier 3 = 4 AP, Tier 4 = 5 AP, Tier 5 = 6 AP; move near = 1 AP, move far = 2 AP; attack with an ally or item = 2 AP; simple interaction (lever, door, pick up, disrupt one siphon line with a check) = 1 AP; maintain a lasting effect = 1 AP per turn. Spell casters also pay attention to distance: a spell against a far target costs 1 AP more than the same spell against a near target (HOUSE RULE, applying the sourcebook's rule that spells pay AP based on distance to target). Card effects that say "Draw a Card" grant +1 AP (sourcebook's card-effect table).

Practical advice: a turn with 6 AP usually means one summon plus one attack, or one summon plus two moves, or three attacks with an already-summoned ally — and then the character is done. Players who spend everything on the first turn will be standing still on the second. That is a feature, not a flaw; let them learn it in Scene 2, where the stakes are survivable.

### Allies in combat (sourcebook)

Allies have HP and Damage but no set Defense — they use their controller's Command as Defense. The summoner rolls Command for an ally's attacks. Allies vanish at 0 HP, and healing is possible before they vanish. If an ally was carrying an item, the item remains behind for others to pick up; magical buffs tied to the ally fade at once. If a controller stops maintaining a summon, the ally disappears. Keep allies' HP visible — a small pile of tokens beside each ally card works.

### When to award Soulrocks (sourcebook)

Soulrocks are gained on critical successes, or when the GM judges a moment significant for character growth or pleasing to the Powers — on average 1–3 per player per session. In this adventure, award at most one Soulrock per player per scene for a significant moment, on top of any earned by critical successes, and say why out loud ("That bargain with Kess pleased Reyer — take a Soulrock"). Soulrocks are stored as d6s placed in a stat (sourcebook); remind players they can spend them between scenes (costs are on the cheat sheet below) but not in the middle of a fight, except the 1-Soulrock death postponement.

### When to use Clarity and Strain (sourcebook)

- Clarity (d8 modifier, cannot crit-fail): a practiced task, a tool used properly, a plan the party prepared, a character acting in their element. Use it generously in Scene 1's social play and for Willa's herb work.
- Strain (d4 modifier, cannot crit-succeed): exhaustion, fear, injury, fighting in smoke or darkness, a character pushing past their limits. Use it in the grotto once the siphon is running, and for any character who has already taken a Summoner's Bargain wound this encounter — strain should feel earned, not arbitrary.

### Death at 0 HP and the Summoner's Bargain (sourcebook)

When a character reaches 0 HP, the player chooses: die (with final words, and a minor final act of 2 AP or less if combat allows); pay 1 Soulrock to postpone the decision 1 turn (giving allies time to heal them); or take the Summoner's Bargain — lose a random card permanently and stabilize at 1 HP, OR take a lasting wound / penalty and stabilize at 1 HP. For the wound option, roll on the restored d12 penalty table (see the cheat sheet). Severity worsens with repeated 0 HP events in one encounter — the first Bargain is survivable, the third may be beyond saving. Record Bargains on the character sheet. Let the player choose, always; never choose for them. In this one-shot, if a player loses a card to the Bargain, the GM should offer a clear path to earn an equivalent card back in a continuation session (sourcebook allows this).

### Rest and recovery (sourcebook)

Health Dice by Max Health: 1–3 HP none (Vulnerable), 4–7 d4 (Fragile), 8–11 d6 (Average), 12–15 d8 (Resilient), 16+ d10 (Exceptional). Short rest (1 hour) rolls 1 Health Die, long rest (8 hours) rolls 2, extended rest (24+ hours) rolls 3, and the total rolled is HP regained. There is one safe long-rest point in this adventure: camp at the Watcher's Shelf after Scene 2. Everywhere else, rests are short and interrupted.

## Opening scene: dusk in Alderford

Read aloud:

> The road west ends at Alderford the way all good roads end — in a village that did not expect you, and is glad you came anyway. Thatch roofs, a stone well, smoke from chimneys, and the smell of baking bread from the storehouse oven. The children are supposed to be indoors by dusk, but two of them are still out by the well, daring each other to look west, toward the mountain.
>
> West is the Great Crater. Even from here you can see its rim — a bright ring against the darkening sky, like a lantern left burning on a high shelf. Except tonight the ring is dimmer than it should be. And the well water, when you cup a hand to taste it, has a taste like old iron.
>
> An old woman with a walking staff is watching you watch the mountain. "You saw it too, then," she says. "Good. The village needs people who see things. Come to my hut before the light goes. We have a great deal to talk about, and not much night left to talk in."

GM notes: The old woman is Hedda, Warden of the Rim (ally; see `Characters/Hedda-Warden.md`). Do not rush this scene. Let each player introduce their character in one or two sentences — name, vessel, and one thing they are carrying that is not a Form (a bedroll, a carving knife, a spare cloak). Award no Soulrocks here; this is arrival, not achievement. If a player asks about the dimming, Hedda answers honestly: she does not know the cause, she knows the signs, and she knows where the signs point — up the rim trail, to the old grotto. Move to Scene 1 when the party accepts her invitation or asks for the trail. If they refuse both, Hedda does not argue — she leaves a lantern burning in her hut window all night, and Tamsin (below) will raise the same matter in the morning, with the added news that the storehouse well has gone iron-tasting too. The hook returns; it never dead-ends.

## Scene 1 — Alderford: the flicker, the storehouse, and the rival (social)

Location: [[Alderford]]. NPCs present: Hedda (ally), Tamsin, Keeper of the Storehouse (support), Kess Varro (rival).

### Read-aloud / setup

> Hedda's hut is one room, warm, and full of maps drawn on hide and bark — trail lines worn pale by fingers, springs marked with blue thread, and one place, high on the crater's rim, circled three times in charcoal. "The Hollow Heart," she says, tapping it. "An old grotto. Wardens used to shelter there. Something has moved into it, or something has woken in it. The lights along the rim are going out one by one, like candles in a draft. The springs taste of iron. The wild things are frightened, and frightened wild things bite."
>
> She looks at each of you in turn. "I can take you to the Watcher's Shelf, where the old tower stands. Past that, the trail is yours. Bring rope. Bring light. Bring someone who can talk, because you may meet someone up there who would rather bargain than fight — and you may meet someone who would rather fight than bargain. Either way, do not go into the grotto without light."

### What is here

- **Hedda's knowledge (free, for asking):** the rim trail, the Shelf tower, the grotto's two entrances (the main passage, and a narrow chimney above it that only a small ally or a nimble climber can use), and the warning that summoned allies flicker near the grotto — a sign the shard itself is being drained.
- **Tamsin's storehouse (supplies, for fair dealing):** rope, lantern oil, dried food, spare blankets, and healing poultices. See Treasures below. Tamsin also knows the village gossip: a well-dressed stranger (Kess) has been asking about shards, and a traveler sold her a map of the rim trails two nights ago.
- **Kess Varro's arrival (mid-scene, unannounced):** Kess walks in as the party is outfitting, sizes them up, and offers a partnership — she will guide them past the Shelf's blocked trail and split whatever the grotto holds, "fairly, and in writing, if you like." She is lying about the fairness and telling the truth about the blocked trail. See the social encounter below.

### Social encounter: dealing with Kess

Kess's stats are in `Characters/Kess-Varro.md` and her Forms in `Cards/Kess-Forms.md`. This is a social encounter first; it becomes a fight only if the party forces it, and Kess will not fight to the death here — she has a camp to get back to and a purse to protect.

- **Command (negotiation) vs. Kess, Standard (5):** a success gets her to admit the blocked trail is real and that she wants the shard for a buyer, not for herself. A critical success gets her to name the price she was offered — information that matters in Scene 3.
- **Command (read emotions) vs. Kess, Standard (5):** a success reveals she is nervous about something she is not saying (the buyer — see her secret in her sheet). Do not reveal the secret itself here; reveal only that there is one.
- **Imagination (political history / bargaining sense) vs. Kess, Hard (7):** a success lets a player spot that her "in writing" offer has a gap — it splits the grotto's contents, but not the shard itself. Naming the gap aloud makes Kess laugh, genuinely, and respect the party a fraction more.
- **If the party accepts a partnership:** Kess travels with them to the Shelf and proves useful (she knows the blocked trail's bypass), then parts ways at the Shelf with a warning: "Past here, we are rivals again. Fair warning is more than most get." This is the best outcome for information and the worst for trust — play it that way.
- **If the party refuses or insults her:** Kess leaves with a bow and a smile, and the party will meet her again at the Shelf, where she has the advantage of arriving first. Nothing is lost except goodwill, and goodwill with Kess is a currency the party may wish they had kept.
- **If the party attacks her in the village:** Hedda and Tamsin intervene verbally, not physically — "Not in Alderford. We settle debts on the road, not in the storehouse." The fight, if it must happen, happens on the road in Scene 3, not here. Do not let a bar brawl eat the session's first hour.

### Treasures and Soulrock awards (Scene 1)

- **Storehouse outfit (free, for asking fairly):** each character takes rope (one coil), lantern oil (one flask), dried food for two days, and one village poultice. A village poultice restores 2 HP when applied (1 AP, simple interaction) and is then gone — an adventure item rule, stated here so the whole table uses the same number.
- **Hedda's trail map:** a copy of her hide map for the party's navigator. HOUSE RULE: while the party follows Hedda's map on the rim trail, navigation checks (Essence / Imagination) are made with Clarity (d8 modifier die).
- **Soulrock award:** 1 Soulrock to any player who secures a genuine concession from Kess (the price, the gap in the contract, or a partnership on honest terms), or who talks the party out of a village fight. At most one per player in this scene.

### On success / failure

- **Success (party leaves equipped and informed):** the trail to the Shelf is open, Kess is a known quantity, and the party knows about both grotto entrances. Scene 2 runs as written.
- **Failure (party leaves unequipped, uninformed, or hostile):** never a dead end. Hedda catches them at the village edge with a spare coil of rope and the one sentence they most need ("The chimney entrance is above the main passage — small things fit, people do not, unless they climb"). Kess, if hostile, still leaves first and becomes Scene 3's problem rather than Scene 1's. The trail is always walkable; it is only ever harder without preparation.

## Scene 2 — The rim trail: the Shelf, the slope, and the crag hounds (exploration and combat)

Location: the trail from Alderford to [[Watchers-Shelf]]. Travel is on foot, uphill, through pine and then bare rock. The rim ring grows brighter as the party climbs, and then — near the Shelf — visibly thinner, like a lamp running low on oil.

### Read-aloud / setup

> The trail narrows to a shelf of pale rock, and there stands the Watcher's tower — roofless, its stair gone to rubble, its beacon dish rusted open to the sky. Below it, the trail drops toward a spring that should be running clear. It is running rust-red. And between you and the spring, picking their way down the scree with their hackles up, are three hounds made of stone and bad temper, their eyes lit from inside by the same thin light as the rim.
>
> They are frightened. Frightened things, Hedda said, bite.

### Exploration: the loose slope

Before or after the fight (the party's choice, but the slope must be crossed to reach the spring), the party faces a loose scree slope above the trail.

- **Crossing carefully (Finesse, Standard 5):** each character rolls once. Success crosses cleanly. Failure means a slip: the character slides to the bottom, takes 1 damage, and arrives with Strain on their next check (their footing is shaken, not their courage).
- **Crossing with rope (Tomas's rope and grapnel, or the storehouse rope):** HOUSE RULE: a character using a secured rope rolls with Clarity (d8 modifier die) on this crossing.
- **Finding the safe line (Essence tracking / Imagination navigation, Standard 5, one roll for the party by its best tracker):** success finds a goat path around the worst of the scree; everyone after the finder crosses with Clarity.
- **If the whole party fails:** they still get down — sliding, bruised, 1 damage each, and arriving noisily, which means the hounds below are alerted and the fight's Center starts on the hounds rather than on the spring. A harder fight, not a blocked trail.

### Combat: the crag hounds

Enemies (full WR stats, plain listing — no separate sheet needed):

- **Crag Hound, pack leader — HP 7, DEF 3, Damage 2, AP 6 (HOUSE RULE)**
  - Pools: Might 3d6 (max 3), Finesse 3d6 (max 3), Command 2d6 (max 2), Essence 2d6 (max 2), Imagination 1d6 (max 1), Craft 1d6 (max 1)
  - Attacks with Might (bite and stone claws), 2 AP per attack (HOUSE RULE). A single-target critical success doubles its damage to 4 (sourcebook).
- **Crag Hound, packmate (two of them) — HP 6 each, DEF 3, Damage 2, AP 6 (HOUSE RULE)**
  - Pools: Might 3d6 (max 3), Finesse 2d6 (max 2), Command 1d6 (max 1), Essence 2d6 (max 2), Imagination 1d6 (max 1), Craft 1d6 (max 1)
  - Attacks with Might, 2 AP per attack (HOUSE RULE).

- **Center of Combat:** the spring (the objective the hounds are guarding and the party needs to reach). Whoever is closest to the spring acts first. If the hounds are alerted by a noisy descent, the Center starts on the pack leader instead — the fight is about the leader, not the spring, until the leader falls or flees.
- **Center shifts:** if the pack leader drops to 2 HP or fewer, it flees up the scree and the Center shifts to the remaining hounds; if a player reaches the spring, the Center shifts to that player (they now hold the objective, and the hounds must come to them).
- **Tactics:** the hounds fight as a pack — the leader holds the center, the packmates harry flanks. They do not use Forms, they do not retreat as a group, and they do not pursue past the spring. A hound reduced to 0 HP crumbles into ordinary stone and a fading wisp of rim-light; it is a corrupted thing ending, not an animal dying, and the GM should say so, so the party does not carry guilt they did not earn.
- **Nonviolent alternative (always available):** a character may spend their turn attempting to calm the pack instead of attacking — Essence (handle wild creature) vs. the pack leader, Hard (7), with Clarity if Willa (or any Essence-led character) offers food first. Success calms one hound, which sits and watches; two successes (two characters, or one critical success) calm the pack, and the party may pass and use the spring freely. This is slower than fighting and costs no HP — reward it exactly as you would reward winning the fight.

### What the spring tells the party

A character who examines the spring (Essence natural history or Imagination magical awareness, Standard 5) learns: the water is not poisoned, it is drained — the iron taste is what water tastes like when the living light has been pulled out of it. A character who examines a crumbled hound (Craft searching or Imagination magical creature, Standard 5) finds a fleck of lodestone grit in the stone — the same material as Corvin's circlet, though the party cannot know that name yet. Both clues point one way: something below the rim is pulling light out of the mountain. Jory's trail-sense (his Verdant affinity) confirms it without a roll if he is present: the energy underfoot runs downhill, toward the grotto, the wrong way.

### Treasures and Soulrock awards (Scene 2)

- **The Shelf camp:** the tower's lee is a safe camp. A long rest here (8 hours) rolls 2 Health Dice (sourcebook). This is the adventure's one safe long rest — say so plainly, so the party uses it.
- **Spring water (once restored, after the climax):** not usable yet. Do not let the party "fix" the spring here; the drain is below, not at the spring.
- **Soulrock award:** 1 Soulrock to any player who calms the pack without a fight, crosses the slope without any party member slipping, or lands the blow that ends the fight while protecting a wounded ally. At most one per player in this scene.

### On success / failure

- **Success:** the party camps at the Shelf, rests, and wakes to a clear trail to the grotto. Kess, if she is ahead of them, has left a polite note at the camp ("Gone ahead. Try not to fall in. — K.") and a small, genuinely useful gift: a spare flask of lantern oil.
- **Failure (party driven back or badly hurt):** they fall back to Hedda's hut, rest, and return the next morning with Hedda herself walking them to the Shelf — she will not go further, but her presence means the hounds, if any remain, do not re-engage, and the slope is crossed with her guidance (Clarity for all). The cost is a day, not the adventure.

## Scene 3 — The Watcher's Shelf: the rival's choice (social, with an optional fight)

Location: [[Watchers-Shelf]] and the rim camp above it. NPC present: Kess Varro (rival), with her ally Zoroark (see `Cards/Kess-Forms.md`: HP 8, Damage 3, Tier 3, summon 4 AP; it defends with Kess's Command, 4).

### Read-aloud / setup

> Morning on the Shelf, and the rim is thinner still. Kess is already there, sitting on the tower's fallen lintel with her boots off, as if the mountain were her own front porch. Beside her, half in shadow and half not, sits something tall and fox-shaped that is smiling at you in a way you do not entirely trust.
>
> "You made it," Kess says. "Good. That saves me the trouble of coming back for you. Now — we can do this the hard way, where we race for the grotto and one of us gets hurt, or we can do this the clever way, where you tell me what you found at the spring and I tell you what I found at the grotto mouth. I went down far enough to hear it, you know. The siphon. It hums."

### The negotiation

Kess will trade: what she heard at the grotto mouth (the siphon hums, there are three lines running from it, and the man inside talks to himself in two voices — his own, and something colder) for what the party learned at the spring (the water is drained, not poisoned; the hounds carry lodestone grit). This trade is genuinely useful — it gives the party the siphon's structure (three lines) before the climax, which is the difference between a plan and a scramble.

- **Command (negotiation), Standard (5):** a straight trade, information for information, no strings.
- **Command (negotiation), Hard (7) or a critical success:** Kess also admits she is uneasy about her buyer — "a purse that large, for a shard no one has seen, from a man who will not give his town" — which is the thread that, pulled in the grotto antechamber, turns her against the villain (see Scene 4).
- **If the party lies to her:** she knows — her collector's eye (see her sheet) spots it — and she laughs it off, but the trade is off. She leaves, and the party enters the grotto without the three-lines knowledge. They can still win; they will simply discover the siphon's structure mid-fight, which is a fine dramatic shape too.
- **If the party offers her a share of the shard:** she is tempted, honestly tempted, and refuses — "A share of a drained shard is a share of nothing. Get it whole, and then we will talk about shares." This refusal is a clue: she does not want the crater dead either.

### The optional fight

If negotiation fails badly, or the party chooses to force the issue, Kess fights — to win position, not to kill.

- **Kess Varro — HP 11, DEF 4 (5 while her Thief's Cloak is maintained), AP 6 (HOUSE RULE)**
  - Pools: Command 4d6 (max 4), Imagination 3d6 (max 3), Might 2d6 (max 2), Finesse 4d6 (max 4), Craft 3d6 (max 3), Essence 2d6 (max 2)
  - She attacks with Finesse (a short blade) for 2 damage, 2 AP per attack (HOUSE RULE), or commands Zoroark (2 AP, rolled with Command).
- **Zoroark — HP 8, Damage 3, DEF = Kess's Command (4) (sourcebook: allies use controller's Command as Defense), AP 6 (HOUSE RULE)**
  - Attacks 2 AP, rolled with Kess's Command.
- **Center of Combat:** the tower lintel (the high ground Kess holds). Whoever holds the lintel acts first. If the party takes the lintel, the Center shifts to the party member holding it.
- **Center shifts:** if Zoroark vanishes, the Center shifts to Kess herself; if Kess retreats, the Center follows her down the trail and the fight is over — do not chase a retreating rival into a second fight.
- **Retreat condition:** Kess retreats (using her Smoke Bomb, see `Cards/Kess-Forms.md`) when she drops to 4 HP or fewer, or when Zoroark vanishes — whichever comes first. She leaves a parting line, not a corpse: "You fight well. The man in the grotto fights worse, and with worse reasons. Remember that."
- **If the party defeats her outright (she reaches 0 HP):** she does not die by default — the GM should offer her the same choices a player gets (she may pay the Bargain's price in story terms: she loses Zoroark's Form permanently and stabilizes at 1 HP, humbled and, for the first time, honest). Do not kill a named rival in Scene 3 of a one-shot unless the table has explicitly chosen that shape of story.

### Treasures and Soulrock awards (Scene 3)

- **Kess's information (the trade):** the siphon's three lines, the hum, the two voices. Worth more than any item in this scene.
- **Kess's parting gift (if the trade was fair):** a spare flask of lantern oil and a chalk mark on the party's map showing the chimney entrance's exact position.
- **Soulrock award:** 1 Soulrock to any player who closes the trade honestly, talks Kess down from a fight, or wins the fight without reducing Kess below 4 HP (restraint, counted as a significant moment). At most one per player in this scene.

### On success / failure

- **Success:** the party enters the grotto knowing its structure and with Kess as a possible late ally (see Scene 4).
- **Failure (no trade, fight lost, or Kess driven off hostile):** the party still enters the grotto — the trail does not close. Kess, if hostile, will appear in the climax to take the shard in the confusion rather than to help, which is a complication, not a dead end. If the party lost the fight and retreated, they rest at the Shelf camp (long rest, 2 Health Dice) and try the grotto the next morning; Kess, having won, has gone ahead and is already inside, which changes the climax's opening (she is arguing with Corvin when the party arrives — let the party listen for one round before acting).

## Scene 4 — The Hollow Heart: the siphon and the scholar (climax)

Location: [[Hollow-Heart-Grotto]]. NPCs present: Corvin Ash (villain; see `Characters/Corvin-Ash.md` and `Cards/Corvin-Forms.md`), and possibly Kess Varro (depending on Scene 3).

### Read-aloud / setup

> The grotto is not dark. That is the first wrong thing. It is lit — lit the way a room is lit when someone has stolen all the lamps in the village and put them in one place. Light pools in the center, around a rig of iron and chalk: three dark lines running from a cracked stone set in an iron circlet, out to the walls, and down, down into the mountain, drinking.
>
> In the middle of the light stands a man in scholar's robes, pale, grey-veined hands, eyes like wet stone. He does not startle. He has been expecting someone, and he is politely disappointed it is you.
>
> "You are here about the dimming," he says. "Of course you are. Everyone comes about the dimming. Sit — no, you will not sit. Very well. Then listen, before you break something you do not understand. The shard is failing on its own. I am the only one keeping it alive. What I take, I take to preserve it. What you would do — leave it here, in the dark, to die — that is the theft."

### The antechamber (before the fight)

Before the main chamber, the party passes an antechamber with two things worth finding (Standard 5 checks, Craft searching or Imagination magical awareness):

- **The warden's marker:** Hedda's carved trail marker from decades ago, still standing. A character who asks Hedda about her first trip (Scene 1) recognizes it at once. It marks the safest approach: a side passage that enters the main chamber behind the rig, near the siphon lines, rather than through the front where Corvin's wards face.
- **The scorched first circle:** the remains of Corvin's first harness attempt, deliberately cracked. A character who examines it (Imagination, Hard 7) understands: the lodestone did not crack by accident. This is the fact that, spoken aloud to Corvin, makes him falter for one full turn (see his sheet's secret) — the party's one scripted opening, usable once.
- **The villain's mark on a contract:** if the party dealt fairly with Kess and told her about her buyer in Scene 3, or if they search here, they find a copy of Kess's contract bearing Corvin's mark. Showing it to Kess (if she appears in the climax) turns her against Corvin for real.

Do not let the antechamber eat the climax's time. Two checks, two findings, then the main chamber. If the party skips the antechamber entirely, they enter through the front and the fight starts harder — still winnable, still fair.

### The bargain (one round, before dice)

Corvin opens with a bargain, not an attack: leave, and he will pay the party in shards — small, true shards, enough to make each of them comfortable. He is sincere. If the party accepts, the adventure ends badly and quietly: the crater keeps dimming, and the GM should say so plainly, then offer the party the chance to reconsider before they leave the mountain (a second bargain, this time from Hedda's memory: "A man who pays you to leave a dying thing is not preserving it"). Accepting the first bargain is a valid ending, but it is an ending the table should choose with open eyes, not drift into. If the party refuses, Corvin sighs, and the fight begins.

### Combat: Corvin and the siphon

- **Corvin Ash — HP 13, DEF 4, AP 6 (HOUSE RULE)**
  - Pools: Command 5d6 (max 5), Imagination 4d6 (max 4), Might 2d6 (max 2), Finesse 2d6 (max 2), Craft 4d6 (max 4), Essence 3d6 (max 3)
  - Attacks with his Frostbrand Spell (see `Cards/Corvin-Forms.md`): 3 damage while the siphon runs (2 base + 1 siphon bonus), 2 damage once the siphon is broken. Summon cost 3 AP (HOUSE RULE). A single-target critical success doubles the damage actually dealt that turn (sourcebook).
  - He also commands his allies (2 AP per ally attack, rolled with Command).
- **Houndoom, Corrupted — HP 9, Damage 4, DEF = Corvin's Command (5), AP 6 (HOUSE RULE)**
  - Attacks 2 AP, rolled with Corvin's Command. The front-line threat; it does not retreat.
- **Gengar Shade — HP 7, Damage 3 (2 once the siphon is broken, HOUSE RULE), DEF = Corvin's Command (5), AP 6 (HOUSE RULE)**
  - Attacks 2 AP, rolled with Corvin's Command. It slips along walls and strikes from cover.
- **Center of Combat:** the lodestone rig (the objective). Whoever is closest to the rig acts first. This is deliberate — the fight is about the rig, and the turn order should push the party toward it.
- **Center shifts:** if Corvin leaves his warded circle to chase a party member, the Center shifts to Corvin; if a party member grabs the shard (after the siphon breaks — see below), the Center follows the carrier, and Corvin's allies turn toward the carrier.
- **The siphon lines (the fight's real objective):** three lines run from the lodestone rig to the walls. A character adjacent to the rig may disrupt one line per turn: a simple interaction (1 AP, HOUSE RULE) plus a successful Standard (5) Craft or Imagination check. Each disrupted line is one step toward breaking the siphon. Three disrupted lines break it. Breaking the siphon removes Corvin's +1 spell damage, drops the shade's damage to 2, and stops the grotto's draining light — the room visibly brightens, and the party's allies stop flickering. Describe that brightening; it is the moment the party knows they are winning.
- **Strain in the grotto:** while the siphon runs, any character who has already taken damage in this fight rolls with Strain (d4 modifier die) on checks made inside the warded circle (HOUSE RULE, applying the sourcebook's Strain condition: the adventure taking its toll). Once the siphon breaks, Strain lifts.
- **Corvin's surrender:** if Corvin drops to 3 HP or fewer AND the siphon is broken, he surrenders. He does not beg; he sits, carefully, and says, "Then it dies with me, or lives without me. You have chosen. Finish it kindly." The party must decide what to do with him — that decision is part of the ending, not an afterthought.
- **If Kess appears:** if the party dealt fairly with her, she arrives two rounds into the fight to harry Corvin's flank (Zoroark attacks the shade, Kess attacks the rig's wards). If the party crossed her, she arrives two rounds in to take the shard in the confusion — she grabs for it, not for a kill, and the Center shifts to her. Either way, she does not stay for the ending; she leaves with a line and, if she helped, a nod.
- **The shard, when the siphon breaks:** the crater shard — a crystal the size of a fist, warm, bright, and clearly alive in a way no cut gem is — rests in the rig's cradle. Taking it is a simple interaction (1 AP). Whoever takes it becomes the Center of Combat. The shard itself does nothing in this fight except shine; what it does afterward is the party's choice (see Endings below).

### Endings (the party chooses; the GM does not)

- **Return the shard to the crater (Hedda's hope):** the party carries the shard to the crater floor's lake edge at dawn and sets it in the water. The rim ring brightens, the springs run clear, the hounds' stone crumbles to ordinary rock, and the village's well tastes of water again. Hedda weeps, once, and pretends she did not. This is the cleanest ending and the one the village will remember.
- **Keep the shard, guarded (the party's trust):** the party keeps the shard under joint guard, promising to return it if the dimming returns. The crater stabilizes but does not fully brighten — a compromise with a cost, and a strong continuation hook (who guards it, and who comes looking for it).
- **Give the shard to Kess (the bargain's price):** if the party trusts Kess's buyer story no longer, they will not do this — but if they do, Kess pays them fairly, leaves, and the buyer (Corvin's middleman, unknown to her) becomes the next adventure's villain. The GM should not hide this consequence after the fact; Kess's contract mark, found in the antechamber, is the warning, and a party that ignores a warning owns the result.
- **Destroy the shard (the desperate choice):** a character may attempt it — Might, Heroic (9), with the shard resisting in a way that feels alive and afraid. Success ends the drain forever and ends the crater's light forever too: the rim goes dark, the village loses its landmark, and the party must answer for that in Alderford. Do not offer this ending unprompted; let a player propose it, and then let the table weigh it.

### Treasures and Soulrock awards (Scene 4)

- **The shard itself:** see Endings. It is not a treasure to sell; it is a responsibility to decide about. If the table wants a mechanical benefit, the GM may grant the shard's carrier 1 Soulrock immediately (a significant moment, sourcebook) — and nothing else. No stat bonuses, no extra Forms from the shard in this one-shot.
- **Corvin's notes:** his scholar's journals, left in the grotto. They explain the siphon, the first circle, and the buyer's network — the paper trail for any continuation. Luz (or any scholar) will want them; let her have them.
- **The warden's marker:** if the party returns it to Hedda, she keeps it, and the party gains a standing invitation to her hut — a safe rest, a warm meal, and true information, any time they return to the rim.
- **Soulrock award:** 1 Soulrock to every player who takes part in breaking the siphon or in Corvin's surrender (a significant moment for the whole party), plus any earned by critical successes in the fight. This is the scene where the session's 1–3 per player average is reached — do not be stingy here.

### On success / failure

- **Success:** the siphon breaks, the shard is secured, and the party chooses an ending. The crater's fate is decided by the party, not by the GM.
- **Failure (party driven out of the grotto):** they retreat to the Shelf camp, rest (long rest, 2 Health Dice), and return — Corvin, weakened by the party's first attempt (the GM reduces his HP by the damage already dealt, and one siphon line stays disrupted), is still there, still polite, and slightly less certain. A second attempt is a new fight, not a repeat of the first.
- **Failure (party wiped out / all at 0 HP):** use the Summoner's Bargain rules, not a total-party kill, unless the table has chosen otherwise. A party that Bargains its way out of the grotto, carrying wounds and missing cards, has a better story than a party that simply ends — and a stronger reason to return.

## Continuation hooks (2–3, for the next session)

1. **The buyer's network.** Corvin's notes name a middleman and a distant town that buys true shards. Kess's contract was only one thread. Someone, somewhere, is still paying for crater shards — and now they know the party has one, or had one. The next adventure is a road story: follow the money, or wait for the money to come to Alderford.
2. **The shard's keeper.** If the party kept the shard, or returned it only partly, the crater's light is now the party's responsibility in a practical sense: wardens, scholars, and shard-hunters will all come to Alderford asking about it. The next adventure is a village story: who guards the light, and what does guarding it cost?
3. **Corvin's second chance — or second attempt.** If Corvin surrendered and lived, he is a scholar without a project and a man with a debt to the mountain. He may seek the party's help to repair what he broke (redemption, and a knowledgeable, untrustworthy ally), or he may vanish with a fragment of the shard and try again somewhere quieter (a returning villain who knows the party's tactics). The GM should choose based on how the party treated him at the surrender — mercy earns the first, cruelty earns the second.

## GM cheat sheet (one page — keep this open, not the rulebook)

### Difficulties (HOUSE RULE)

Easy 3 | Standard 5 | Hard 7 | Heroic 9. Check total = pool successes (d6s succeed on 4+, 6s explode and reroll, chaining) + modifier die number.

### Modifier die and criticals (sourcebook)

Default d6; Clarity d8 (cannot crit-fail); Strain d4 (cannot crit-succeed). Rolling max = critical success, min = critical fail. In combat, a single-target crit = double damage; otherwise crits at minimum definitely succeed (success) or fail (fail).

### AP costs (HOUSE RULE)

6 AP per turn, refreshing at the start of the character's turn. Summon Tier 1 = 2 AP, Tier 2 = 3 AP, Tier 3 = 4 AP, Tier 4 = 5 AP, Tier 5 = 6 AP. Move near = 1 AP, move far = 2 AP. Attack with an ally or item = 2 AP. Simple interaction (lever, door, pick up, disrupt one siphon line with a check) = 1 AP. Maintain a lasting effect = 1 AP per turn. Spells against a far target cost +1 AP. "Draw a Card" = +1 AP (sourcebook).

### Turn order and Center of Combat (sourcebook)

No initiative. Name the Center (person, objective, or hazard); closest to it acts first. Ties: highest remaining AP, then d6 roll-off, then narrative logic. The Center shifts when the fight's focus shifts — announce every shift.

### Allies (sourcebook)

Allies have HP and Damage, no set Defense — they use their controller's Command as Defense. Summoner rolls Command for ally attacks. Allies vanish at 0 HP. Items persist only while maintained; a destroyed item is simply dismissed.

### Health Dice and rest (sourcebook)

1–3 HP: no Health Die (Vulnerable). 4–7: d4 (Fragile). 8–11: d6 (Average). 12–15: d8 (Resilient). 16+: d10 (Exceptional). Short rest (1 h) = roll 1 Health Die; long rest (8 h) = 2 dice; extended rest (24+ h) = 3 dice. HP regained = total rolled.

### Soulrock costs (sourcebook, restored tables)

Max HP: 2 Soulrocks per 1 HP. Card Pool: 5 per 1 slot. Dice pool max: 4 per 1 point. Feats + Abilities: 9 per ability. Adjective Upgrades: 9 per adjective. Card upgrades: 3 Soulrocks × Max Card Tier. Defense Style and Card Tier MAX scale across upgrades 1–4: 5 / 9 / 12 / 15 Soulrocks. Soulrocks gained: critical successes or GM-judged significant moments, average 1–3 per player per session.

### Death at 0 HP and the Summoner's Bargain (sourcebook)

Player chooses: die (final words, and a minor final act of 2 AP or less if combat allows); pay 1 Soulrock to postpone the decision 1 turn; or take the Bargain and stabilize at 1 HP — either lose a random card permanently, or take a lasting wound / penalty (roll d12 below). Severity worsens with repeated 0 HP events in one encounter.

Bargain penalty table (d12, restored from the sourcebook, verbatim): 1 Coma — 1d6 hours, breakable by healing but extremely difficult. 2 Severe Mobility Loss — lose both legs; needs a wheelchair, mount, or magical movement aid. 3 Major Sensory Loss — lose both eyes; must rely on a familiar or allies to navigate. 4 Dominant Arm Lost — attacks and physical actions requiring two hands cost +1 AP. 5 One Eye Lost — slight perception loss, disadvantage on long-range attacks. 6 Nerve Damage — −1 to Craft. 7 Large Magical Scar — purely aesthetic, glows faintly with chaos energy. 8 Weakened Essence Flow — −1 to Essence. 9 Fractured Psyche — −1 to Arcane (editor's note in the sourcebook: Arcane is not one of the six WR stats and likely means Imagination, which covers understanding complex magic — the GM decides before rolling). 10 Unstable Connection — summoned allies cost +1 AP for the next three sessions. 11 Unwanted Mutation — a chaotic physical change (extra fingers, glowing veins, a voice that echoes unnaturally). 12 Strange Resilience — avoids major damage but permanently gains a supernatural quirk (never sleeps, cannot drink water, learns a new language).

### Card tiers and conversion (sourcebook)

Pokémon TCG numbers operate in the tens: divide by 10, consistently for every card. Tier 1 usually ≤3 damage and ≤5 health. Tier 2 around 4–6 damage and <7 health. Tier 5 has no limits. Card-effect interpretations: Draw a Card = +1 AP (Draw 2 = +2 AP); offensive buffs = +1 to the attack roll OR +1 to damage (player chooses, unless a Forms file fixes it); defensive buffs = +1 DEF, damage reduction, or temporary HP (temporary HP usually most balanced); poison = 1 damage at the start of the target's turn unless stated otherwise; confusion = harder to hit (disadvantage or reroll failed attacks); sleep / stun = target misses turns or loses AP, with a roll each round to break free.
